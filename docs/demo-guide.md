# Demo guide

A runbook for showing Firefly to a team, a mentor or a panel in about 15 minutes. It says what to show, what to say and what not to claim. It describes what the repository does today. If you change the set-up, change the script. The times are a plan, not a measurement.

## Before you start

1. **Install and check.** You need Node.js 24 or later. Run `npm ci`, then `npm run check`. It runs the format check, lint, type check, scenario validation, tests, build, size budgets, secret scan and lockfile check. Fix anything that fails before the day.
2. **Pick a set-up and say which one you are using.** Put settings in `.env` (copy `.env.example`). Firefly reads `.env` when it starts, so restart after a change.

3. **Rehearse the scenario.** The script uses _Colleagues cannot join scheduled Teams meetings_: the customer is Priya Raman at Tailspin Toys, the call is a first contact, and the preset is Standard, so the practice mode starts as Assessment practice. It is the first card in the carousel, so it is already chosen when the scenarios page opens. Play it once so you know what the customer says. The wording changes from call to call. To show Guided practice, choose **Guided practice** under **Practice mode** for the same scenario, or try _Shared team mailbox missing from Outlook_ (Starter), where it is the default. In Guided practice the case panel adds "Areas to explore" and a "Context found" count, a thin trace shows how the customer has felt, a tips strip ("During the call") sits under the transcript, and **End call** opens "Ready to close?" with four neutral checks (plan, owner, update time and the customer's agreement) beside the usual actions. Assessment practice shows none of these. Use a laptop-sized window: one that is both narrower than about 1,024 px and no taller than about 704 px leaves the strip out (see [Showing Guided practice](#showing-guided-practice)).
4. **Reset after the rehearsal.** Restart the server. Scripted speech takes its sentences in order from one list for the whole server process, so a rehearsal moves the starting point. Delete the rehearsal calls (see [After the demo](#after-the-demo)), or set `FIREFLY_DB_PATH=:memory:` for a database that disappears when the server stops. The browser tests use that setting.
5. **Start the app.** For development, run `npm run dev` and open <http://localhost:5173>. For a production-style run, run `npm run build`, then `npm start`, and open <http://127.0.0.1:8787>.
6. **Check the room.** A quiet room and a headset help with voice. Allow the microphone for the address in advance if the browser remembered a "block". Keep the brief page open so you can switch to a text call at once.
7. **Close what you do not need.** Other tabs, notifications and anything else that uses the microphone.

In demo mode the customer's reply is delayed by 700 ms on purpose (`FIREFLY_MOCK_DELAY_MS`) so the "Customer is replying" state is visible. It says nothing about speed.

### What to expect in demo mode

The customer opens with a greeting and does not say why they called until you have greeted them. A customer with something urgent at stake, as in the Teams scenario, adds a line about it. They give a fact only to a question that fits it. A dismissive line raises the mood. If nobody has taken ownership, a request to speak to a manager can follow. A promise with no time ("I'll get back to you soon") gets a question about when. A clear owner and time is said back as a question or taken in with a few words. "Is there anything else?" and a goodbye are answered in a few words, with nothing new raised. Owning the problem with a time calms the customer. The words differ from call to call. See [customer-realism.md](customer-realism.md) for the rules.

In a spoken call, hands-free listening is on by default: the microphone opens by itself after each customer reply. With scripted speech, every recording that reaches the server (150 ms or more) uses up the next scripted sentence, whatever it holds. Keep the room quiet while the customer replies, or turn **Hands-free** off in **Settings** and press **Talk** for each turn. The microphone also stays live while the "End the call?" or "Ready to close?" dialog is open, so press **Pause** before **End call**.

### Showing Guided practice

The two practice modes are chosen on the scenarios page: Starter starts as Guided practice, and Standard and Challenge start as Assessment practice. The case brief names the one in use, and it cannot be changed once the call has started.

- **Guided practice** adds a tips strip headed "During the call" under the transcript, "Areas to explore" and a "Context found" count in the case panel, and, under the customer's state, a thin line labelled "State so far" that shows how they have felt, with no numbers (it is left out of a window narrower than about 1,024 px). Ending the call opens "Ready to close?".
- **Assessment practice** has none of these, and ending the call opens "End the call?". The case panel still shows the Objective row. It is a practice mode with no tips, not an assessment: its report is the same practice signal.
- **The tips** show one card and a peek of the next. A card changes only when you move it (**Previous tip** and **Next tip**, the arrow keys, Home and End, or a drag with a mouse, finger or pen) and once when the customer has finished a turn. It never changes on a timer or while the customer speaks. **Learn why**, on most tips, opens one more line in place: the strip grows and nothing is covered, and Escape or a press outside closes it. **Hide tips** puts the strip away and **Show tips** brings it back; nothing is saved, so a reload shows the strip again. The tips are general advice, chosen only from the call stage and how many messages you have sent, never from the scenario's hidden facts.
- **Where the strip is left out.** A window that is both narrower than about 1,024 px and no taller than about 704 px has no strip, and on a narrow window it steps aside while the on-screen keyboard is open. Use a laptop-sized window for a demo.
- **"Ready to close?"** opens with the line "What the call has covered so far. This is not a score." It lists four rows read from the call so far by rules alone: Plan stated, Owner stated, Update time stated and Customer agreement confirmed. Each says Done or Not yet, and only a covered row is picked out, with a green tick. It never says what to say. If it is slow or fails it is left out, and **Continue call** and **End and review** are always there.

### Lines to type in demo mode

In a scripted run of this scenario in demo mode, with these lines, the customer gave the reason after the first line, gave up facts to the scoping questions, rose in mood after the dismissive line, eased after the recovery line and answered the closing question in a few words. The rules engine matches patterns in words, so a line with unusual wording may not be recognised. Use them as a guide, not a script.

- **Greet:** "Hello, this is Sam from Microsoft support. How can I help you today?"
- **Empathise and open up:** "I'm sorry the meetings are failing, that sounds stressful with the presentation coming up. What exactly happens when someone tries to join?"
- **Scope:** "Who is affected, and does it happen with every meeting or only some?"
- **Ask about changes:** "Has anything changed recently, for example a policy or a setting?"
- **Dismissive, to show the mood rising:** "Calm down, it's probably something your team did."
- **Recover and own it:** "I'm sorry, that came out wrongly. I will take ownership of this and call you back by three o'clock with an update. Is there anything else I can help with today?"

## How demo mode is labelled

Say this once, early. Demo mode is never presented as a model.

- On the scenarios, brief and report pages the header shows "Demo mode" (a compact "Demo" chip below 768 px wide, which opens to the full words). A live model shows "Live:" and its name instead. The landing page header shows neither.
- On the brief, **How your voice is handled** has a row "Who plays the customer", which says "Demo mode: the customer is played by Firefly's built-in rules engine."
- On the call page, the last pre-flight row, "Customer AI", reads "Demo mode: Firefly's built-in rules engine". The call bar shows a "Demo mode" badge (a live model shows "Live model") from 360 px wide up; below that, **Settings** has the same words in its "Customer AI" row.
- On the report, the "Demo mode" badge sits by the title. **About this report** says "The customer was played by" Firefly's built-in rules engine (demo mode), not a language model, and that the coaching was written by Firefly's built-in rules evaluator (demo mode).
- Scripted speech is labelled "Demo speech (scripted)" on the brief and the pre-flight page.

## What not to claim

- **Speech recognition.** Do not say it is accurate, enterprise-grade or tested with Azure. Azure AI Speech has not been run live. Browser recognition returned no words under automation. The scripted demo speech understands nothing.
- **The customer's voice.** Do not say it shows real emotion. Pace and pitch are adjusted, and a style for a few voices. The result has not been judged by a person.
- **The scores.** Do not say they are accurate, fair or comparable between people. They are practice signals, and they have not been compared with mentors' scores. Assessment practice is only the mode with no tips: its scores are practice signals too.
- **The rules engine.** Do not call it AI or a model. In demo mode there is no language model, and the app says so.
- **Live models.** Do not say one has been evaluated. Gemini had a limited smoke check, which says nothing about voice, other scenarios or paid-tier behaviour, and it was not repeated for the newest customer prompt (`customer-4`) or its guards. Azure OpenAI and the OpenAI-compatible endpoint have not been run live.
- **The scenarios.** The customer and organisation are fictional and the product is real. Do not say a mentor or an engineer reviewed them, or that they come from Microsoft's internal material: the records mark 11 of the 16 as checked against current public Microsoft documentation and 5 in part, all 16 as reviewed by an AI assistant and none by a mentor. The internal wiki was not available.
- **The tips.** Do not say they react to the customer or the scenario. Only the call stage and the number of messages you have sent choose them.
- **CBCF.** Do not say Firefly is aligned with it. The mapping is not applied.
- **Interrupting.** Do not say you can talk over the customer. There is no barge-in.
- **Accessibility.** Do not claim more than automated checks: axe in the browser tests, and a contrast test of the colour tokens in both themes. No screen reader or other assistive technology has been used to test it.
- **Browsers and devices.** The interface was checked in Microsoft Edge. Firefox, Safari and real touch hardware have not been tried.
- **Deployment.** Do not say it is deployed, secure for real data or ready for production. There is no hosting configuration and no sign-in. It is a prototype for a trusted machine.
- **Data.** Never enter real customer information.

## After the demo

Stop the server with Ctrl+C in its terminal. Calls are kept in `data/firefly.db` (git-ignored) unless you used `FIREFLY_DB_PATH=:memory:`. To delete one call, open its report, choose **Delete this call**, then **Delete call**. To remove them all, delete the database file after the server has stopped. If you used a live model, remove or replace the key if it was ever shown on screen or pasted anywhere.
