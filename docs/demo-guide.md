# Demo guide

A 20-minute runbook for showing Firefly to mentors, reviewers or a panel. The demo is **unscripted**: nothing is pre-written for any conversation you might have, and everything on screen is real except the sample sessions, which are labelled as samples.

## Before you start (10 minutes, once)

1. Install and build: `npm ci`, then `npm run build`.
2. Start the server: `npm start`. A quiet console is nicer on a shared screen: `FIREFLY_LOG_LEVEL=warn npm start` (set the variable first on Windows with `$env:FIREFLY_LOG_LEVEL='warn'`).
3. Open <http://127.0.0.1:8787> in a clean browser window. A fresh browser profile starts with an empty history and no profile.
4. Choose **Begin**, enter a first name and choose **Create profile**. That creates a local profile in this browser: no account, no password.
5. Check the badge beside the wordmark at the top left. **Demo mode** means the built-in rules engine plays the customer and writes the coaching. If you configured a live model it says **Live model**, and **Provider status** on the Trainer page names it. Say which one you are showing; never present one as the other.
6. Open **Studio** in the dock; it opens on the **Trainer** tab. If you want a safety net, choose **Load sample sessions** under **Demo data**. They are pre-generated by the built-in engine, always labelled "Sample", never earn points, and are kept out of your own progress unless you switch on **Include sample sessions** on Progress.
7. Do one practice call yourself so you know how the spotlight scenario behaves.
8. If you want to show voice, use Chrome or Edge on localhost, and turn it on first with **Enable voice (Beta)** under **Settings > Preferences** (see below).
9. **Ctrl or Cmd + K** opens a palette that goes to any page, starts practice, finds a mission by name and switches the theme. The **Pause ambient motion** button at the top right stills the animated background if it distracts on a shared screen.

If you use a live model, run the smoke test in [ai-providers.md](ai-providers.md#optional-manual-smoke-test) first, and keep demo mode ready as the fallback.

### The call: things worth trying

You are the trainee. Type in your own words. These are suggestions for showing particular behaviours; the customer reacts to what you actually write.

- **Empathy changes the mood.** Open with something specific: "I'm sorry this is holding up your deliverable." The customer's tone softens (in guided practice the mood word, the presence and the field behind the page change with it, and in either mode the words do).
- **Hidden facts need the right questions.** Ask what changed recently, what exactly the screen says, whether anyone can still open it, and what has already been tried. Each unlocks something the customer would not volunteer. A vague "can you tell me more?" does not.
- **Blame and jargon hurt.** For contrast, try "You should have read the instructions" or a stack of technical terms with a novice customer. The customer reacts, and the report will say why.
- **A curveball.** Keep to questions and do not take ownership for the first few turns. In the spotlight scenario the customer may ask for your manager. Notice how you handle it.
- **Ownership.** Then say what you will do and when: "I'll take this on and call you back at 2pm with an update."
- **Guided and immersive.** Starter calls default to guided practice and show light cues (the customer's mood in a word, a count of signals found, a few **Ideas** above the message box that only fill it, and areas worth exploring under **Details**). Standard and Challenge are immersive with none of that, and the presence stays neutral so it never gives the mood away. Say that the cues are deliberately subtle so a trainee does not play the numbers.
- **Voice (Beta).** Turn it on under Settings > Preferences (read out what the dialog says about audio), then press **Voice call** under the message box. Say plainly that Firefly never records audio, that Chrome and Edge usually send speech to their maker's service, and that typing always works.

### The report: what to point at

The sections run in this order down the page, with a numbered rail beside them.

1. **Masthead and summary.** The scenario and customer, how the call ended and who produced the coaching (demo mode or a model), the customer held still as they were at the end, and the whole call as one line. Then two or three plain sentences with a quote from the call, beside the overall score, the hidden facts found, the customer's mood range and the coaching focus.
2. **Customer temperature.** Turning points are labelled and each is tied to what the trainee said. Walk them with the arrow keys; **Show as table** gives a table alternative for screen readers.
3. **Curveball.** If one landed: what happened and how it was handled.
4. **Six skill scores.** A practice signal, not a grade or a ranking. The constellation is also the way into each skill, which shows the rationale and the quotes behind it. Click a quote's **Turn** link to jump to that turn in the transcript, or **See it in the conversation** to read the turns around it. Short calls are labelled as resting on limited evidence.
5. **What went well** and **Opportunities to improve.** Each opportunity has a stronger way to say it and why it works.
6. **Discovery.** Which hidden facts were found, partly found or missed, the question that revealed each, what the customer had held back and why it mattered. This only appears after the call.
7. **Rewards from this call.** The points the call earned and why. A call that ends early earns none, and it says so.
8. **Next practice target.** One focus and a suggested scenario.
9. **Report incorrect feedback.** Under **About this report**. Show the dialog: it copies a note for a mentor with no transcript text in it.

Remind the audience: this is practice coaching, not an assessment. A mentor's review adds what automatic feedback cannot.

## If something goes wrong

| Problem                                            | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The live model is slow or fails                    | Say so plainly. Firefly shows a safe message and a Retry; a saved message is never lost or duplicated. A free-tier Gemini project allows only about 15 requests a minute per model (as tested), so type at a human pace. If it keeps failing, restart with `FIREFLY_PROVIDER=mock` and continue in demo mode, labelled as such. |
| You need data for History, Progress or Calibration | **Studio > Trainer > Load sample sessions.** Say they are a labelled backup dataset generated by the built-in engine.                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| You want a clean slate                             | **Studio > Trainer > Delete my practice sessions**, or **Reset everything**. Each asks for confirmation and says what will be removed.                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| The report is slow to appear                       | The wrap-up screen shows the real stage. In demo mode it takes a moment; with a live model it can take longer. The transcript is always safe.                                                                                                                                                                                   |

## Questions you may be asked

- **Is it scripted?** No. In demo mode the customer is a rules engine that composes replies from the scenario's facts, the customer's state and the trainee's words. In live mode a model writes the replies from the same constraints.
- **How do you know the scores are right?** We do not claim they are. Quotes are verified against the transcript, but the scores are only as good as the rules and need calibration against mentors. No calibration results exist yet; the tooling does.
- **Can the model leak hidden facts?** Hidden facts the trainee has not earned are never put in the model's prompt, and replies are scanned for details that appear only in an unearned fact. It is a safety net, not a proof.
- **Where does data go?** A local SQLite file. With a live model, transcripts go to the provider you configure (with Gemini on a free-tier key, Google's terms say it may use and human-review what is sent, so keep everything fictional). There is no telemetry.
- **Has the live model path been tested?** Against a real service only for Gemini, in a few scripted calls (see [ai-providers.md](ai-providers.md#verified-against-the-gemini-api)). Azure OpenAI and other OpenAI-compatible services have been tested only against a local fake server.
