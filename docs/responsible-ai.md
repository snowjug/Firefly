# Responsible AI and data safety

Firefly is a **training aid**. It gives apprentices a safe place to practise customer conversations and get feedback they can act on. It is not a tool for judging people. The main points, in short form, are on the Responsible AI page inside the app, which also has short notes on the practice tips, the closing check, Better wording and where the scenarios come from (under "AI customer and coaching", in "How the AI and coaching work"). This page has the detail behind them.

## What Firefly is for, and what it is not

| It is for                                                                   | It is not for                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Practising realistic conversations with a simulated, fictional customer     | Assessing, ranking or comparing colleagues                          |
| Getting feedback tied to what you actually said, with quotes from your call | Performance reviews, promotion decisions or any employment decision |
| Discussing a call with a mentor, who adds what automatic feedback cannot    | Replacing mentor review                                             |
| Repeating a scenario at a different difficulty                              | Training on or storing real customer cases, logs or screenshots     |

Scores are practice signals from a simulated call. Firefly has no leaderboard, no ranking, no points and no progress tracking, no screen that lists calls or scores together, and it never compares one person with another. A report shows that call's own scores and nothing else. The report words its overall figure the same way: "Practice signal 4.2 of 5, the average of six skills" (4.2 is an example), and its Summary section carries the note "Automatic practice feedback, not a performance assessment." For a call of two trainee turns or fewer it shows no number: it says "No overall practice signal: not enough evidence." unless the summary already says so.

## Safeguards in the product

**Fictional by design**

- Every scenario uses invented people, organisations and products. The library tests fail if a scenario contains an email address, a phone number or a link.
- The interface labels the customer as fictional. The footer on the landing, scenario, brief, report and information pages says "A training aid. Customers and scenarios are fictional." The scenario details, the case brief, the pre-flight page and the call screen say "Synthetic customer", the case brief is tagged "Synthetic scenario", the case reference in the call is tagged "Synthetic", the report's About section marks the customer "(synthetic)", and the Responsible AI page tells you to keep every call fictional.

**Where the scenarios come from**

- The scenarios are fictional cases about real Microsoft products. Their wording was compared with current public Microsoft documentation (Microsoft Learn and Microsoft Support pages) by an AI assistant on 22 November 2026. That review was not a mentor's, and it was a reading of public pages, not a test on a live service. The internal Microsoft supportability wiki was not available to this project, and nothing in a scenario is taken or paraphrased from it.
- Each scenario carries an internal record of when it was written, which public source categories it used, whether its wording was checked (11 are `checked-current` and 5 are `checked-partly`) and that the review was an assistant's. The record is never sent to the browser, to a model or to a log. See [scenario-authoring.md](scenario-authoring.md#provenance-grounding).
- A scenario describes what a customer would report, in everyday words. It is not a statement of how a product behaves, and it can still be wrong.

**Less personal data**

- Obvious personal data in a trainee's message (email addresses, phone numbers, long numbers, identifiers) is replaced with a marker before the message is saved or sent to any model. This runs on recognised speech as well as typed text, after recognition. It is pattern matching and will not catch everything, and it cannot touch audio, so the safest approach is to say and type nothing real.
- There is no account, no password, no analytics, no tracking and no telemetry. One anonymous cookie keeps a browser's calls apart. Logs hold short session ids, codes and timings, never transcripts or audio.
- The browser keeps at most two small preferences in `localStorage` and sends neither anywhere: the theme, once you choose Light or Dark (`firefly.theme`), and whether you have dismissed the first-use microphone notice (`firefly.voice-notice-seen`). The footer's theme choice says "Saved on this device" only when the browser kept the choice, and "Not saved: storage is blocked" when it did not. See [privacy-audio.md](privacy-audio.md).

**Voice**

- A call can always be typed. A spoken call asks for the microphone only after an explicit click (the optional **Check microphone** on the brief, or **Start voice call** on the pre-flight page), and the brief and the pre-flight page say before that where your speech will be processed.
- Firefly never writes audio to disk, logs it or stores it. With a server speech provider (Azure AI Speech) the recording goes through Firefly's server to the provider and is held in memory for one request. With the browser's own recognition, the audio is handled by the browser, which in Chrome and Edge is usually the browser maker's online service; Firefly does not record or send it. With the scripted demo provider, the audio is discarded. See [privacy-audio.md](privacy-audio.md).
- The customer's voice is synthetic: a prebuilt voice from the browser or the provider, not a real person, not a clone and not an imitation. The trainee chooses between four neutral voices, labelled by pace and never by a person, a gender or an accent. Pace and pitch (and, for a few voices, a style) are adjusted to suggest the customer's mood. No test or measurement here checks how that sounds, and Firefly does not claim it shows real emotion.
- Firefly does not analyse your voice for emotion, stress, tone, accent or pace. Coaching reads the words only. Speech recognition can mishear words, so the Transcript section of a report on a call that used voice says "Spoken turns were transcribed automatically and may contain recognition errors."

**Practice help (Guided practice only)**

- Guided practice adds help to the call that Assessment practice does not have: a strip of general tips under the heading "During the call", the "Areas to explore" list and a "Context found" count in the case panel, a thin line under the customer's state that shows how they have felt so far (no numbers; not on a narrow screen), and a neutral closing check when you press End. Assessment practice has none of these: its end dialog is "End the call?" with no check.
- The tips are fixed, general advice about running a support call, such as "Establish impact before troubleshooting." (`web/src/features/tips/tips.ts`). Which tip comes up depends only on the call stage and how many messages you have sent, and on which tips you have already seen. No tip is chosen from, or written about, a scenario's hidden facts, the customer's mood, the product or an unexpected turn. Tests read every tip against every scenario's hidden facts and fail on a word or a run of words they share, and check that the tips code cannot import a scenario or the state of a call (`tips.test.ts`, `tips-source.test.ts`). They check wording, not meaning, so they cannot prove that no tip could ever nudge a trainee. "Hide tips" puts the strip away and "Show tips" brings it back. The strip is left out of a window that is under 64 em wide and no more than 44 em high (704 px at the default text size), and while the on-screen keyboard is open.
- "Context found" is a count of what you have found so far: never a total and never what it was. It counts the hidden facts the customer has said, in full or in part, so Assessment practice leaves it out: a count that ticks up while you ask would be a cue about what the customer is holding back (`web/src/features/call-ui/CaseContext.tsx`).
- The closing check ("Ready to close?") opens with "What the call has covered so far. This is not a score." and shows four rows, "Plan stated", "Owner stated", "Update time stated" and "Customer agreement confirmed". A covered row is marked "Done" with a tick, and the others say "Not yet" in quiet text. It is neutral: not a score, not shown as an error, and it never says what to say. Rules read the call so far (`server/evaluator/closingCheck.ts`), with no model, nothing is stored and nothing in the report reads it. It never blocks ending the call: "End and review" stays available, and if the check is slow (more than a second) or fails, it is left out without a message.

**Control of the model**

- In live mode, a model only ever receives the facts the engine has decided the customer may share in the current reply. Hidden facts the trainee has not earned are never in the prompt, apart from a gentle clue the scenario's author wrote for a fact, which the customer may drop at Starter difficulty. Replies are scanned for details that appear only in an unearned fact. See [ai-providers.md](ai-providers.md#how-hidden-facts-are-protected).
- The customer, the turn classifier and the coaching evaluator are separate steps with separate instructions, so the customer's role cannot be changed by what a trainee types. Attempts to make the customer break character or reveal instructions are recognised and answered in character.
- A model never decides the customer's state. Customer state, discovery and curveball timing are computed in code, and every model output is validated before it is used. When a live model drafts the coaching, it proposes the six skill scores and the server checks them against the evidence; CARE behaviours, milestones, discovery and the overall score are always computed in code. Text that came from a model is shown as plain text, never as HTML.
- API keys, for the model and for speech, live only in the server's environment. They are never sent to the browser or written to logs.

**Honest feedback**

- Every quote in a report is checked against the turn it cites. A quote that does not appear there is removed before a report is saved.
- **Better wording is wording to try, not a script.** Each improvement in a report puts what you said (a quote that is checked against your turn) above a stronger way to say it, labelled "Try", with the words that changed marked as removed and added. The stronger wording is generated: the built-in rules evaluator builds it from templates that use details of the scenario and the call, and in live mode the model drafts it under a prompt that says it shows a behaviour and is not a script. The server cleans a model's draft and keeps it to a word budget, but nothing checks that it is better. It is one suggestion for that moment, not a phrase to repeat on every call. Each block opens with "Wording to try for this call, written by the coaching. Use your own words.", and the About this report section says who wrote the coaching ("Coaching written by"). When the quote cannot be matched to your turn, the "You said" line says "Insufficient evidence" instead of any words, and nothing is marked.
- A score needs evidence: a 4 needs a verified quote and a 5 needs two. A skill that no verified quote backs is shown as a "first look" (dashed segments, no rubric word such as "Strong", and the label "Limited evidence"). A short call says its report rests on limited evidence instead of inventing a full assessment, and a call of two trainee turns or fewer shows no overall score.
- Scores do not go down because of short messages, a terse style, grammar or spelling, an accent or non-native phrasing, or a communication style that differs from the reviewer's own ([care-cbcf-rubric.md](care-cbcf-rubric.md#what-is-never-scored)).
- Demo mode is labelled wherever the provider shows. The call bar says "Demo mode" or "Live model" (from 360 px wide; below that the bar has no room for it). The "Customer AI" row in Voice settings and on the pre-flight page says "Demo mode: Firefly's built-in rules engine", or "Live model:" and the provider's name. The report has a "Demo mode" badge, and its About this report section says who played the customer and who wrote the coaching ("Firefly's built-in rules engine (demo mode), not a language model", or "the language model" and its name). Replies and feedback from the built-in rules engine are never presented as coming from an AI model.
- The scripted demo speech provider is labelled "Demo speech (scripted)" on the pre-flight page and in Voice settings. Voice settings say "Scripted demo: it does not understand audio.", and the pre-flight's customer-voice row reads "A demo sound, not a voice".
- CBCF mapping is not applied, and the report says so. No CBCF material was found in this repository or supplied for it, so Firefly makes no claim of alignment with it ([care-cbcf-rubric.md](care-cbcf-rubric.md#cbcf)).

**Human review**

- A mentor reading the transcript will see things the report cannot. Every report carries a note, in its About this report section, that a mentor's review adds what automatic coaching cannot. Firefly has no mentor tooling, and this repository holds no comparison of its scores with mentors' scores. No scenario has had a mentor's review either: all 16 scenario records say `assistant-reviewed`.

## Limits you should know about

- **Automatic coaching can be wrong.** The rules evaluator reads patterns in text, so it can miss a good way of saying something or read a sentence too generously. A model-written evaluation can be fluent and still mistaken. Quotes are shown so a person can check what a score rests on.
- **Tone of voice is not assessed.** Coaching reads the words that were said or typed. Warmth, pace, accent and pauses cannot be assessed, even when voice is used.
- **A simulation is not a customer.** A scenario has fixed facts, and the demo-mode customer is rules-based and less flexible than a person or a model.
- **Scenario wording has had an assistant's review, not a mentor's.** It was read against public Microsoft pages, with no access to the internal supportability wiki, and some of it could not be confirmed (5 of the 16 scenarios are only partly checked). A scenario is a plausible story, not a statement of how a product behaves.
- **English only**, and nothing here has been tested for how well it works for every kind of speaker. Non-native phrasing must never be marked down; if you see it happen, treat that as a bug.
- **Speech is only partly verified.** Azure AI Speech has never been run against the live service, and nothing has been tested with real speech or real recognition. The scripted demo speech works end to end in the browser tests, which run in Chromium with a fake microphone. See [voice-architecture.md](voice-architecture.md#verification).
- **Live models have had little testing.** Gemini had one limited smoke check on 21 Nov 2026 (two scripted calls; no errors, no retries, and no coaching or list-style replies found by simple automatic checks). That is a smoke check, not a quality evaluation: it says nothing about voice, other scenarios or paid-tier behaviour. It ran before the customer prompt became `customer-4`, so the live model has not been exercised with the newest prompt or its newest guards: those have met only a fake model. Azure OpenAI and OpenAI-compatible services have been run only against a local fake server. None of this says how good a model's coaching is. See [ai-providers.md](ai-providers.md#status-of-each-provider).
- **Accessibility has not been checked with assistive technology.** See [accessibility.md](accessibility.md) for what was checked and what was not.
- **No certification.** Firefly makes no claim of compliance or alignment with any standard, framework or regulation, including CBCF, and nothing here is legal advice.

## For people who run Firefly

- **Where data lives.** In the default setup, everything is in a SQLite file on the machine that runs the server (`data/firefly.db`, git-ignored) and is not encrypted by Firefly. If you use a live model, redacted transcript text is sent to the provider you configure, and if you use a speech provider, recordings and the customer's lines go to it, so choose and configure them with your organisation's data rules in mind.
- **Free-tier model services may learn from what you send.** Google's Gemini API terms (effective 23 March 2026, read on 21 November 2026) say that, for unpaid services, Google uses the content you submit and the responses to improve its products, that human reviewers may read it, and that sensitive, confidential or personal information must not be submitted; terms differ by plan and region. Firefly sends only fictional scenario text and redacted trainee messages, so type nothing real. Read the provider's current terms before connecting any key. See [ai-providers.md](ai-providers.md#gemini-google-ai-studio).
- **Tell trainees which speech set-up is active.** The brief and the pre-flight page show it to them. Speech-to-text of something sensitive said aloud is processed by the provider you chose.
- **Keep API keys out of Git.** Keys belong in the git-ignored `.env`. If one is ever pasted into a chat, a ticket or a commit, treat it as exposed: delete it at the provider and create a new one.
- **Who can see it.** Firefly has no accounts: the anonymous cookie separates one browser's calls from another's on a shared server, but it is not authentication, and anyone with access to the server can read its database. Keep Firefly on a trusted network, or put sign-in and TLS in front of it.
- **Deleting data.** A call can be deleted from its report page ("Delete this call"). Deleting the database file removes everything.
- **Using it well.** Use scores to start a conversation with a mentor. Do not use them for evaluation, and do not publish individual results.
