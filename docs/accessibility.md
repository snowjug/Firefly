# Accessibility

Firefly is a practice tool for people who are learning a job, and some of them use a keyboard, a screen reader, a magnifier, voice control or a phone. This page says what the interface is built to do, how it is checked, what has **not** been checked and where it is known to fall short. Paths start at the repository root.

## What this page claims

- The target is **WCAG 2.2 level AA**. It is a target, not a claim of conformance. Firefly has not been audited by an accredited body.
- The only checks are **automated**: axe-core runs in the component tests and in the browser tests, and the browser tests also read focus, landmarks, headings, live regions and layout. These find the defects a rule can describe.
- **No screen reader or other assistive technology has been used to test Firefly.** What a screen reader says is inferred from roles, names and live regions that tests read, not from listening. See [What was not checked](#what-was-not-checked).
- This page records no pass count and no run date. A result belongs to a commit; CI runs the browser tests on every change (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`).

## What is built in

Every item below is in the code. Where a test covers it, the test is named.

### Structure and navigation

- The document is `<html lang="en">` (English, not a regional variant), declares both colour schemes (`<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">`) and asks for `viewport-fit=cover` (`web/index.html`).
- The first Tab stop on every page, the call page included, is a **Skip to main content** link (`web/src/app/shell/Shell.tsx`). `main` has the id `main` and can take focus.
- Landing, information, scenario, brief and report pages have a header, `main` and a footer. The call page has no header or footer: it draws its own command bar inside `main`, and adds a **Jump to** list (the call controls, the transcript and, in a typed call, the reply box) (`web/src/features/call-ui/JumpLinks.tsx`).
- The report has section navigation: **On this page** on wide screens, with the current part marked by `aria-current="location"`, and a **Jump to** disclosure on narrow ones (`web/src/features/report/SectionNav.tsx`).
- The report's call journey and its CARE review are tab patterns: one Tab stop for the row, the arrow keys, Home and End choose, and the panel is a Tab stop of its own. The journey is a row of moments only where every moment fits in one row (about 80 px each, from 48em up); on a narrow screen, or for a call with so many moments that they would not fit, it is a list of buttons (`aria-expanded`) that open their turn in place, one at a time, with the arrow keys, Home and End moving between them and opening left as a deliberate act. A moment says its place in words: "Turn 6", "After turn 9" for the end of a call that stopped early, "Not reached", or "Insufficient evidence". The four CARE principles are one row of tabs at every width: when they do not fit it scrolls sideways with the chosen tab kept in view, and each tab says in words how many of its behaviours were shown. A turn number in the transcript is a toggle button that marks the turn and selects the nearest moment, and says so politely; the turn numbers are one Tab stop for the whole transcript, and the arrow keys, Home and End move between them (`Timeline.tsx`, `Care.tsx`, `Transcript.tsx`).
- A quote in the report is shown only when the turn it cites says it; otherwise the page says "Insufficient evidence". In the CARE review each principle shows one quote, never the same words under two principles, and a behaviour that repeats a quote says "The same words as above" with a link to the turn. The better wording marks what was removed and added with `<del>` and `<ins>` (struck through and underlined, with the words "Removed:" and "Added:" for a screen reader), so it never depends on colour. A line above it says what it is ("Wording to try for this call, written by the coaching. Use your own words."), and its visible key ("Struck through: removed", "Underlined: added") is hidden from assistive technology, because the marked words carry their own (`BetterWording.tsx`, `Care.tsx`, `evidence-model.ts`).
- The customer-experience chart in the report is a group named "Customer temperature through the call". Its name carries a sentence about the whole call and says that the same information is listed below the chart. Its points are buttons with one Tab stop for the whole chart: the arrow keys, Home and End move between them, and Enter or Space shows the exact turn under the chart and says "Showing turn 6, under the chart." politely. Each kind of point has its own marker shape and is named in the chart's key. A line above the chart reads out the point that is pointed at or focused, and a footnote under it says that the customer is simulated, so the heights show direction and not exact measurements; the "Calmer" cue beside the axis is for the eye and is hidden from assistive technology. The same points are listed as "Important points" (each with the customer's reply, "Their reply, turn 7") and, one press away, in a table under the chart (**Show as a table**) with a caption, column headers, the turn as a row header and a link to the turn on each row (`JourneyChart.tsx`, `Experience.tsx`).
- A link to evidence ("See turn 6", named "See turn 6 in the transcript") is a real link. It scrolls to the turn so that it lands about a third of the way down the window, clear of the sticky bars and of the **Back to ...** control (a turn too tall for that starts just under the bar), without gliding when motion is reduced, and a person who scrolls for themselves takes the scrolling over. It moves focus to the turn, marks the turn and the quoted words for four seconds and remembers where it came from, so **Back to ...** returns focus to the same place. The links are 24 px high with a mouse and 44 px under a finger. If the transcript has no such turn the words are plain text and not a link (`web/src/features/report/evidence.tsx`, `dom.ts`, `report.css`).
- The six scores are a list of rows. Each row has the skill, its score as five segments with the number and the word as text, how many quotes back it, and a button named in full ("Opening & Rapport 4 of 5 Strong 2 quotes Expand details") that opens the rest of the reason, the quotes and what to improve in place (`aria-expanded`). A score that no quote backs is a first look: its segments are dashed, its word is "first look" and not a rubric word such as "Strong", and one neutral label, "Limited evidence", stands where the quote count would. **Expand all** and **Collapse all** say "All six skills opened." or "All six skills closed." through the polite announcer (`Scores.tsx`).
- The scenario cards are a carousel: a region named "Scenarios" (on the landing page, "Scenario cards") that holds a standard list of cards, each in a list item that knows its place in the set. Its count, "Selected 3 of 32" (or "1–4 of 12", the range in view, when nothing is chosen), is plain text that describes the region and is not a live region; the position bar beside it, which follows the scroll, is hidden from assistive technology. In the workspace each card is a radio in one group, named by its title and described by its product, how it starts, its time and its level; on the landing page each card is a link. Nothing moves by itself: there is no autoplay, no looping and no copy of a card (`web/src/design/carousel/Carousel.tsx`, `web/src/features/scenarios/ScenarioCard.tsx`).
- The product filter above the cards is one radio group named "Product": **All** and one chip for each product, each named with how many scenarios it would show. It scrolls sideways on a narrow screen and never wraps, and Home and End go to its first and last chip (`web/src/features/scenarios/ProductRail.tsx`).
- Browser tests check that each route has its own title, one `h1`, headings that do not skip a level and landmarks that are named without clashing (`e2e/a11y.spec.ts`).

### Focus and keyboard

- Focus is visible: a 2 px outline with a gap, black in the light theme and white in the dark one, and in forced colours a 2 px `Highlight` outline (`web/src/design/base.css`). A scenario card draws the ring round the whole card. The keyboard spec checks that every Tab stop it passes shows an indicator that is in view and has at least 3:1 contrast.
- Lists of choices (the product filter, the difficulty filter, the preset and the practice mode, the theme choice and the scenario cards) are native radio groups, so Tab enters once and the arrow keys move between options. In the carousel of cards the four arrow keys, Home and End move to a card and choose it, and a card that takes focus is brought into view. The landing page's row of cards is a row of links, where the left and right arrows, Home and End move focus only and Tab still reaches every link. The keys stop at the ends and never wrap. Tab then goes on to **Previous scenarios** and **Next scenarios**, which look dimmed at an end but stay focusable (`aria-disabled`), and then to the details. Keyboard tests cover the preset and difficulty groups and the cards (`e2e/keyboard.spec.ts`, `e2e/carousel.spec.ts`).
- The theme can be changed from the keyboard in two places: a header button named "Dark theme" whose pressed state says whether dark is on, and a Light, Dark and System group in the footer. A choice made in the footer is said once, in words ("Light theme saved on this device.", "Dark theme saved on this device." or "System theme saved on this device."), through the polite announcer, and the footer shows "Saved on this device" for a moment. When the browser blocks storage the choice still applies, but the words say that it is not kept ("Dark theme applied. This browser is not keeping it.", and "Not saved: storage is blocked" in the footer). The header button has no message of its own: its pressed state is the feedback (`web/src/design/theme/ThemeToggle.tsx`).
- Dialogs are native `<dialog>` elements opened with `showModal()` (`web/src/design/primitives/Dialog.tsx`). Focus stays inside, Escape closes, and focus returns to what opened the dialog. A dialog has its own live region, because a modal makes the rest of the page inert. When it closes the dialog itself goes at once and focus returns at once, so a key pressed straight after Escape reaches the page; a fade of about 150 ms (200 ms as a sheet on a phone) is played by a picture of it that is hidden from assistive technology, inert and takes no press, and under reduced motion there is no picture (`e2e/shell-motion.spec.ts`).
- When a control that held focus is replaced (a button swapped for a notice about it, or a notice dismissed), focus goes to the call controls instead of falling to the top of the page. Focus that is somewhere real is never moved (`use-rescue-focus.ts`).
- A hands-free spoken call has no big microphone button, because the microphone opens by itself. The first row of the controls is one slot, always the same height, that holds a single control at a time: **Resume** on a call that is held (the **Pause** button is then not drawn, so there is one of it), **Talk** where the microphone will not open by itself (after four recognitions in a row that found no words, or while a problem card is showing), **Done speaking** while the trainee's speech has been heard, and **Stop customer audio** while the customer speaks. **Done speaking** is a secondary button, never takes focus by itself and leaves once the turn is sent; when a control in the slot that held focus leaves, focus goes to the group of controls (a pause moves it to **Resume**, and a resume back to **Pause**). When the call opens, focus starts on the group of controls, which takes it without being something to press, so the first Tab reaches **Mute**. With hands-free off the controls are as they were: **Talk**, then the same control as **Done speaking** (`call-model.ts` `describeControls`, `Controls.tsx`, `use-rescue-focus.ts`, `e2e/voice-call.spec.ts`, `e2e/keyboard.spec.ts`).
- In the scenario workspace **Start configuration** is pinned to the bottom of the window: across the page below 64em (1024 px) and in the right-hand column from 64em. The controls of the set-up have a scroll margin of 7 rem at the bottom, so the browser scrolls a focused control clear of the bar (`web/src/features/scenarios/detail.css`). Below 360 px the recap beside the button is out of sight but still in the page for a screen reader (`ScenarioDetail.tsx`). No browser test checks the scroll margin.
- There are no keyboard shortcuts of Firefly's own and no single-character keys, so nothing can fire while someone types or uses speech input. In the reply box, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line (`Composer.tsx`). While the customer replies the box is read-only and dim, shows the message that was sent on one line, and says "Waiting for the customer." in its description. The keyboard spec checks that the microphone stops with one key press and that no letter key does anything. The other keys are the standard ones for each pattern, and none is a single character: the arrow keys, Home and End in radio groups, tabs, the carousel, the report's chart and the tips strip, and Escape in dialogs and menus. In the tips strip, while focus is inside it, the arrow keys, Home and End move between tips, and Escape closes "Learn why" and returns focus to its button.

### What a screen reader is told

- One polite `role="status"` region near the root says changes in whole sentences, one at a time. The same sentence is not repeated within four seconds, each is held for 0.7 s plus 25 ms a character (at most 5 s) so it can be read, and at most six wait (`web/src/design/primitives/announcer.ts`). It can lag the screen by a few seconds in a burst of changes.
- The call's transcript is a `role="log"` and is the place transcript text is announced. The customer's reply is written there **before** its voice plays. Every turn carries the time since the call began, written for a screen reader as "at 0:42" after the speaker's name (`Transcript.tsx`). The call timer is `role="timer"` with `aria-live="off"` and is never announced.
- The call's status line comes from fixed words (`describeStatus` in `web/src/features/call-ui/call-model.ts`, which takes them from the voice layer's own in `web/src/features/voice/states.ts`, except that the trainee's own turn reads "Your turn" in a typed call and "Listening to you" in a spoken one). Each state has a sentence for a screen reader, except "Microphone off" (nothing has happened yet) and "Hearing you", which flips many times a call and would talk over the trainee. A refused or missing microphone is announced assertively. A customer voice that failed is announced politely, because the words are on the screen.
- Problems say what happened, what was kept and what to do, with the actions that really apply (**Retry**, **Use text**, **Replay**, **Continue without audio**). See [voice-architecture.md](voice-architecture.md#when-something-fails).
- While the customer works out a reply, the status shows three points with its words and no icon beside them (one cue, not two). The points are decoration; the words "Customer is responding" are in the page for a screen reader, once. The moving bars are hidden from assistive technology (the status line says the same in words). While speech is turned into text the strip shows a shimmer, which is a progress bar named by the status, "Processing speech", with no percentage, and the trainee's turn in the transcript carries a "Transcribing" tag and a placeholder line that is hidden from assistive technology. The thin state trace in Guided practice, which a narrow screen does not draw, has a text equivalent, "Customer state so far: calm, then concerned", and no numbers; its visible name, "State so far", is hidden from assistive technology so that the line is not said twice. Below 360 px the customer's trend is its arrow alone on the screen, and its word is kept for a screen reader (`Waveform.tsx`, `StateTrace.tsx`, `CustomerStage.tsx`, `Transcript.tsx`).
- The customer is a presence, and none of it is for a screen reader. The ring round the avatar (a ring that turns while the call connects, a breath while the microphone waits, a halo that follows the voice, a still dashed ring on hold) is `aria-hidden`, and so is the caption strip under it, which shows the customer's line while it is spoken and the trainee's own words as they are heard. The status line is the words for every state of the ring, and the transcript log is the only place the words are announced, once, when a turn is finished, so a caption never talks over anyone. The captions are text alone (nothing to press, no bubbles), have a height of their own so that nothing moves when they appear, and are left out on a narrow screen, where the transcript is beside the stage and marks the customer's current turn. When the customer's state changes the chip crossfades and sends out one soft ring, which is also `aria-hidden`; the change is said once, in words, by the announcer ("The customer now seems frustrated."), and the ring is never the only sign (`Presence.tsx`, `Captions.tsx`, `CustomerStage.tsx`).
- When End and review is confirmed, the same dialog, named "Ending and preparing your review", shows the call-ended moment: the customer's initials with the ring that surrounded them collapsing, "Call ended", how long the call was (read as "Call length 12:34") and the progress, which says "Ending call", then "Preparing your review", then "Call complete" as the work really goes. The dialog's heading, which assistive technology reads and which takes the keyboard as before, is hidden from the eye so that the moment is not said twice; focus is trapped and restored by the native dialog as for every dialog (`EndCallDialog.tsx`).
- The tips strip of Guided practice is a labelled region, "Tips for this call", with a list of cards named "Tip 2 of 19". It is not a live region and the announcer never reads a tip: a tip changes only when the person moves it, or once when a customer turn has finished (`web/src/features/tips/`). "Learn why" is a disclosure with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` that opens in place, as one more row of the strip: the strip grows by a few lines and the transcript gives way, so nothing is laid over the conversation. Escape or a press outside the strip closes it, and Escape returns focus to the button; another tip arriving closes it too. A tip that is due is let go, not forced on the person, while "Learn why" is open, focus is in the strip or a card is being dragged. "Hide tips" and "Show tips" are outline buttons, and hiding or showing by hand leaves the keyboard on the button that is now there.
- "Ready to close?", the end dialog of Guided practice, opens with one line, "What the call has covered so far. This is not a score.", and a list named "Closing check" of four rows. Each row says "Done" or "Not yet" in words, never in colour alone: a covered row has a green tick and a heavier "Done", the others a grey clock and "Not yet" in regular, quieter text. It says nothing about what to say. While it waits, a skeleton stands in for it, hidden from assistive technology, and it is left out without a message when it fails or is slow. The dialog opens with focus on **Continue call**, so a stray Enter ends nothing, and **End and review** is there from the start and is never dimmed by the check (`ClosingCheck.tsx`, `EndCallDialog.tsx`).
- Controls report their state: **Mute** is a pressed button, disclosures have `aria-expanded`, and a field with an error has `aria-invalid` and the error in its description (`web/src/design/primitives/`).

### Voice, without surprises

- Nothing starts before a click. The microphone opens only after an explicit press: the optional **Check microphone** on the brief, or **Start voice call** on the pre-flight page. The browser test "nothing plays, listens or loads speech before the person starts the call" checks it.
- Typing always works. Every voice error says so, and a typed call hides the voice controls instead of leaving dead buttons.
- The microphone's state is always in words as well as an icon and a waveform. The customer's voice is never the only carrier of what the customer said.
- In a hands-free call nothing is pressed to be heard, so the state has to be plain: the status says "Listening to you", "Hearing you", "Muted" or "Paused" in words, **Mute** and **Pause** close the microphone, and a held call has one large **Resume**. A soft line in the controls' slot, "Still listening. Speak when you are ready.", fades in when it has been quiet for a while, a muted call says "You are muted." there (the mute itself is announced once, as "Your microphone is muted."), and a call that paused itself says why beside **Resume**; the lines are not repeated by the announcer.

### Motion, colour and contrast

- Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, animation and transition durations are cut to 0.01 ms, scrolling no longer glides, the status strip's signal becomes a still level meter, and the three points that show the customer working out a reply stop moving (`web/src/design/base.css`, `web/src/design/tokens.css`, `web/src/features/call-ui/waveform.css`). The shell's own motion has a still form too: a page reached by navigation is simply there (no entrance is declared), a dialog closes with no fade-out, the selection of a segmented control and a tab's line change in place and a press does not compress, a placeholder shows with no wait and no fade, the line across the top of a slow navigation is a still bar, and nothing below the fold is hidden waiting to be scrolled to (`web/src/app/shell/shell.css`, `web/src/design/layout.css`, `Dialog.tsx`; `e2e/shell-motion.spec.ts` checks the page entrance, a dialog's exit and the selection glide with reduced motion on, in Edge, and the rest by their stylesheets). The mouse effects are for a mouse only: a card's lift, tilt and light, the stretch at the end of a drag in the carousel, and in the tips strip the lift, the tilt, the light that follows the pointer and the spring. A touch screen and reduced motion get the plain card, and a tip changes at once. The theme changes without a crossfade. A responsive spec checks that nothing animates for longer than a moment.
- The call screen moves in a few places, and each has a still form under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The ring round the customer turns slowly while the call connects (a whole, still ring), breathes while the microphone waits (a steady halo), follows the trainee's voice, or the customer's where the page can measure it, in steps through a short transition (a steady, brighter halo: a level is never turned into a size under reduced motion), turns quietly while something is worked out (a still ring) and is still and dashed on hold. When the call goes live the avatar eases from a little smaller to full size and one soft ripple goes out, once (neither plays under reduced motion). The customer's state chip sends out one ring when its tone changes, and never when it stays the same. The screen rises in when the call opens, a few pixels and a fade one small step after another, and no control waits for it. Soft guidance and a problem card rise into place and fade; the call-ended moment's ring collapses and its parts rise in, and the report opens at most 0.8 s after End and review is pressed (sooner when the work took longer, and at once under reduced motion). The words of a live caption settle in from a lighter tone as they arrive. Only transform and opacity move, nothing repeats but a state indicator, and `call-motion-source.test.ts` and `web/src/design/motion.test.ts` check the stylesheets (`presence.css`, `captions.css`, `controls.css`, `stage.css`, `dialogs.css`).
- The landing page's example call moves by itself, so it has a button, **Pause preview**, that works from the keyboard and keeps focus. Pressed, it reads **Play preview**, and each press is said once through the polite announcer ("Preview paused.", "Preview playing."). The call also pauses while it is off screen or the tab is hidden, plays three times and then rests on its report, where the button reads **Replay preview** and plays the whole call again, with focus staying on it ("Preview playing again."). It makes no sound and never asks for the microphone. Under reduced motion it shows the finished call and report at once and has no button, because nothing moves (`web/src/features/landing/CallPreview.tsx`, `e2e/landing.spec.ts`).
- The report, the landing page and the scenarios move in a few more places, and each has a still form under `prefers-reduced-motion` and on paper. The report's blocks rise as they are scrolled to, its practice signal and discovery count count up once, its skill bars and discovery segments fill, its chart line is drawn with its markers and one cursor ring that glides between the points chosen, a quote draws its line and the section list's line slides; the landing hero comes in over about half a second, scenario cards that are new to a row arrive (the first eight) and a scenario's details fade in, and "Explore scenarios" glides to the featured row for a moment. Under reduced motion none of it plays and nothing waits to be scrolled to. On paper none of it runs either, and the report prints as it ends: what waits to be seen is hidden or covered only for a screen with motion allowed, the design system's own entrances (a score bar's fill, a disclosure's panel, a message arriving) are declared for a screen only, and the report ends every animation inside it. A number that counts up keeps its real value as the text of the page from the first render and that text never changes, so a screen reader and a printout read it at once; only the drawn digits move, and they are generated content with an empty spoken alternative (`web/src/features/report/count-up.tsx`, `web/src/design/motion.test.ts`, `e2e/report-print.spec.ts`).
- Colour is not the only carrier: customer states, scores and found or missed facts have words or icon shapes as well.
- `forced-colors: active` has its own rules in the component stylesheets (including the carousel's position bar and the theme switch), and `prefers-contrast: more` strengthens borders and `--fg-3` in both themes (`web/src/design/tokens.css`).
- The interface has a light and a dark theme. System, the default, follows the device; a choice made with the header button or the footer is saved in this browser (`localStorage`, key `firefly.theme`) and applied before the page paints, so a saved dark choice does not flash the light page (a browser test reloads the page and samples its background). Both themes pass the contrast tests: in each, 87 text pairs at 4.5:1 and the edges of controls, the focus ring, the waveform, bars and the chart line at 3:1 (`web/src/design/tokens.test.ts`; the ratios are in [design-system.md](design-system.md#contrast-and-accessibility)). Forced colours and print are separate paths: forced colours use the system's colours, and a printed report is always the light document, black on white, even when the app is dark (`e2e/report-print.spec.ts`).

### Targets, zoom and reflow

- The browser tests check that pages reflow without sideways scrolling across widths from 320 to 1920 px (the scenario workspace included, with its carousel and product rail and with the set-up open), that no control is smaller than 24 by 24 CSS pixels where the layout changes, that **End call** and the reply box stay in reach, and that the call stays usable in windows of 320 by 256 px (which stands in for 400 percent zoom of a 1280 by 1024 window), 640 by 512 px (200 percent) and 320 by 300 px (a phone with its keyboard open) (`e2e/responsive.spec.ts`). Text at 200 percent, with wider text spacing, is checked in a 1440 by 900 px window on five pages (the landing page, the scenarios, the brief, the report and a typed call): none may scroll sideways, and the call must keep **End call** and the reply box. Every responsive check runs with a deliberately wide font, so its result does not depend on the machine's fonts.
- The mobile project runs the main journeys on an emulated Pixel 7 (`e2e/mobile.spec.ts`). On an emulated 390 px touch phone the carousel shows one card and about 48 px of the next, fading out, a swipe moves the cards without choosing one, a tap chooses, and **Previous scenarios** and **Next scenarios** are at least 44 by 44 px (`e2e/carousel.spec.ts`). The theme button is 44 px square in its stylesheet. In the tips strip, Previous and Next are 44 px buttons, "Hide tips" and "Show tips" are 44 px high in a phone-sized window (below 40em) whatever the pointer, and under a finger "Learn why" is 44 px too (`e2e/tips.spec.ts`). A narrow window no taller than 704 px has no room for the strip beside the transcript and the dock, so it is left out there (see [Known limitations](#known-limitations)); while the on-screen keyboard is open it steps aside.
- Below a tablet the targets are larger than the 24 px floor. On a 320 px touch phone no control in the scenario workspace (with the set-up open) or in the case brief is under 40 px either way, and **Start configuration** is a full-width button at least 44 px high (`e2e/scenarios.spec.ts`). In a spoken call **Mute**, **Pause**, **Replay**, **Text** and **Settings** are 44 px targets below 640 px wide (`e2e/responsive.spec.ts`). The header's menu button, theme switch and Demo chip are 44 px on a phone, and so are the footer's links and theme choice up to a tablet (`e2e/landing.spec.ts`).

The browser tests run against a production build served by the real Firefly server in demo mode, with an in-memory database, the scripted mock speech provider and a fake microphone that the test controls. They run in Chromium: Playwright's own Chromium in CI, or an installed Edge or Chrome with `PW_CHANNEL`. To run them:

```
npm run build
npx playwright test e2e/a11y.spec.ts e2e/keyboard.spec.ts e2e/responsive.spec.ts e2e/mobile.spec.ts
npx playwright test e2e/carousel.spec.ts e2e/tips.spec.ts e2e/report.spec.ts e2e/report-print.spec.ts e2e/landing.spec.ts e2e/scenarios.spec.ts e2e/text-call.spec.ts e2e/voice-call.spec.ts
```

`npm run test:e2e` builds and runs every browser spec. `npm test` runs the component tests, which include the axe checks.

## What was not checked

- **No screen reader was used.** Not NVDA, JAWS, Narrator, VoiceOver or TalkBack. How a live region queues, whether `role="log"` is read as lines arrive and how a modal is announced differ between screen readers and have not been confirmed. The same goes for the report's counting number, which is built so that its text never changes and the digits that are drawn have an empty spoken alternative.
- **No other assistive technology**: no screen magnifier, switch device or eye tracker.
- **No voice control** (Dragon, Windows Voice Access, Apple Voice Control). Controls have visible text labels and accessible names, but no voice-control tool has been used to drive the interface.
- **No real devices.** Phones are emulated by viewport, and touch is emulated by touch events (a swipe is sent to the page, not made on a screen). Real touch, a real on-screen keyboard, real safe areas and real zoom were not tried, and 400 percent zoom is emulated by a small viewport.
- **One browser engine.** Everything runs in Chromium. Firefox and Safari have not been tried.
- **Layout is measured in the design's own font on a Windows machine, and in a wider one in CI.** Segoe UI Variable is the first font in the stack. The Linux machine that runs CI does not have it and lays pages out in DejaVu Sans, which is wider, so some widths and heights differ there. A few checks have a looser floor for that (230 px of transcript in a spoken call at 320 by 640), and the check that "Done speaking" stays on one line runs only where Segoe UI is installed. Other fonts, such as Roboto or San Francisco, were not tried.
- **One axe scan lets the pinned bars scroll away.** A scan counts a control that is partly under the pinned header or the way-on bar as obscured, wherever the page is scrolled. The scan of the scenario set-up on a phone therefore sets those bars to scroll with the page first, so it says nothing about a control that is under a bar at some scroll position.
- **Forced colours and contrast preferences** are tested with the browser's emulation, not on Windows contrast themes. A dark system setting is emulated the same way, not set on an operating system.
- **The real microphone prompt** cannot be driven by automation, so a fake microphone stands in. Real speech recognition and real speech synthesis were not exercised.
- **No cognitive accessibility review and no testing with disabled people.** The copy is written to be plain and the pages are consistent, but that has not been tested with the people it is for.
- **Automated rules find a fraction of problems.** Whether the reading order makes sense, whether a label is helpful and whether the interface is easy to use need a person.

## Known limitations

- **The announcer can lag the screen** in a burst of changes, because it reads one sentence at a time and holds each long enough to be heard. It is polite and never repeats text, but it is not instant.
- **The tips are not always on screen.** In Guided practice the strip is left out on a window narrower than 64em (1024 px) and no taller than 44em (704 px), for example a phone held on its side, because there is no room for it beside the transcript and the controls. Both limits are in `em`, so they are 1024 px and 704 px at the browser's default text size and larger in pixels at a larger one (that is how a browser reads `em` in a media query; it was not tested). It steps aside while the on-screen keyboard is open. "Hide tips" puts it away for the call; nothing about it is stored, so a reload brings it back.
- **Text at 200 percent in a narrow window was not checked.** The 200 percent text check runs in a 1440 by 900 px window. Narrow widths (320 to 375 px) are checked with text at its normal size, and the call is checked in a 320 by 256 px window that stands in for 400 percent zoom. Enlarged text together with a 320 to 375 px width was not tested, so a page may scroll sideways there.
- **Not every colour pair is measured.** The contrast test checks the pairs it lists, in both themes, and axe scans a page in the state it is in, so a colour that is used only in another state (under the pointer, say) is measured only when its pair is on the list. A link under the pointer is on it (`--brand-fg-hover`); a new colour for a state has to be added to the list by hand. In a browser both themes are scanned with axe on the landing page, the scenario workspace and brief, the Guided-practice call screen and the report; the other pages and states are scanned in the light theme only.
- **`lang="en"`, not `en-GB`.** The text is British English, but the document is declared as English without a region.
- **The interface is English only.** Speech languages are en-GB, en-US, en-IE, en-AU and en-IN.
- **A voice choice may not sound different.** A browser with one English voice cannot make four voices sound different, and the brief says so. This matters for people who rely on hearing the customer, who can read the transcript instead.

## If you find a problem

Treat an accessibility defect as a bug. Add a test that fails without the fix: a component test for structure, or a browser spec for anything that needs layout, focus or a real browser. Update this page if the fix changes what is claimed.
